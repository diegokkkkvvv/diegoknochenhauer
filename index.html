Profile

Background and direction.

I grew up in Querétaro, Mexico. My father worked from home for most of my childhood, and I spent a lot of it next to him, watching him work through long documents and business calls and asking him what every unfamiliar word meant. That is where my interest in business started, well before I studied it.

I attended John F. Kennedy, The American School of Querétaro through graduation in 2024 and completed the International Baccalaureate programme with honors. IB Business Management gave me the structure behind what I had watched at home; IB Economics added the quantitative side; Theory of Knowledge taught me to test my own assumptions and look for the perspective I was missing.

The experience that shaped me most was Culver. I arrived as a cadet in 2022 resisting every part of the military routine and left as a First Sergeant and Operations Officer. I returned as a counselor in 2025 and 2026 to lead a unit of 40 campers of my own.

I chose Hult International Business School for its international focus and practical approach to business. I study Finance with a minor in Economics and am on the Dean's List. Alongside coursework I do independent research in equity valuation, governance and monetary policy.

My goal is to work where leadership and financial judgement meet: in management, operations, banking or investment, and eventually to build and run a business of my own.

Unit Counselor, Naval 4

Culver Summer Schools & Camps, Culver, IN
Summers 2025 – 2026

Every unit at Culver is inspected daily and ranked across the summer on its rooms, its campers' uniforms and bearing, its shared spaces and its marching. I lived with the unit around the clock for six weeks each summer and was responsible for its discipline, scheduling, safety and welfare.

How the unit was run

The unit, known internally as the Sons of Poseidon, was organized into five teams named Foxtrot, Oscar, Romeo, Charlie and Echo. Each team was accountable for its own members, and a shared demerit-tracking spreadsheet made every camper's standing visible to the whole unit.

Before the first inspection, campers and staff went through a structured onboarding session, followed by weekly meetings that reviewed results and set targets for the week ahead. I also ran the unit's identity and communications: branded materials, apparel design, and social media accounts that kept parents informed through the summer.

  • Structure. Divided 40 campers into five accountable teams with a shared demerit-tracking system, so standards were measured daily rather than assumed.
  • Standards. Wrote a Dress A uniform standard operating procedure, cross-checked against the student handbook and correcting gaps on shoulder boards, grooming, ribbon placement and NCO belt requirements.
  • Training. Designed a 22-slide onboarding presentation and a 15-slide weekly meeting format, both with full speaker notes for the counselor team.
  • Communication. Built parent-facing social media and printed materials so families could follow the unit's progress.
  • Results. Finished with the highest camper retention rate of any unit, and in 2026 received the Military Banner, the camp's award for the most disciplined unit based on daily inspections and drill.

Summers 2022 – 2023

My first summer began at 6:00 a.m. with an hour of bed-making, dust-free windows and desks, swept and mopped floors, aligned shoes and an immaculate uniform, all before breakfast. After two difficult weeks I stopped fighting the routine and started leading within it.

What changed

The routine did not change; my approach to it did. I made a point of being the first one ready for every activity, followed the Culver Honor Code, and held myself to the standard I had once thought was impossible. Counselors and other cadets noticed, and the responsibility that followed came from that consistency.

The lessons carried home with me: more discipline, clearer goals, and the confidence to lead people rather than wait to be led.
