About

Two skills that reinforce each other

Modelling tells you what a decision is worth. Leading a team tells you whether it will actually get done.

I'm pursuing a Bachelor of Business Administration at Hult International Business School in Cambridge, MA — Finance major, Economics minor, graduating 2027.

Most of my technical work is self-directed. I build valuation models, stress-test them across scenarios, and pull them apart to see where the assumptions carry the result. Earlier, I chaired Model United Nations, served in student government, and built a reading and writing program that reached more than 40 students.

Research

Three projects, three frameworks

Each one started with a question I couldn't answer from a textbook. Open any to see the method.

The question was whether NVIDIA's valuation could be justified on cash flows rather than sentiment. I built a discounted cash flow model and ran it against a comparable company set, then flexed the growth and demand assumptions to see how much of the equity value depended on the terminal period holding up.

The useful output wasn't a single price target — it was the range, and knowing which two or three assumptions moved it most.

Method
DCF, WACC build-up, trading comparables
Variables tested
Revenue growth, margin path, terminal assumptions
Output
Scenario-banded equity value with sensitivity table
Tools
Excel, Python

I took a corporate governance structure and tested it component by component against the COSO Internal Control framework — control environment, risk assessment, control activities, information and communication, and monitoring.

Gaps are easy to list and hard to rank, so the more valuable half of the work was ordering the findings by the exposure they left open rather than by how simple they were to close.

Using historical rate-cycle data, I modelled how Federal Reserve policy moves transmit into sector equity valuations and into credit conditions — which sectors re-rate first, which lag, and where the effect shows up in financing costs before it shows up in multiples.

It's the piece of work that most changed how I read a discount rate: the number isn't an input you pick, it's a position on a cycle.
